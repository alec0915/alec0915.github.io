Ever since I broke my old Xbox360 Guitar Hero controller, I've been lacking some Guitar Hero in my life. Online prices for these controllers is way too high for my liking, so instead of buying one, I decided to make one instead. It couldn't be that hard to do, and truthfully it wasn't. I purchased some key switches, a generic usb encoder, and some nuts and bolts and got to printing.

<img src="/pictures/guitar0.jpg">

The body came in two pieces and screwed together pretty seamlessly.

<img src="/pictures/guitar1.jpg">
<img src="/pictures/guitar2.jpg">

With the body together, I began the wiring process. I hadn't touched wiring or soldering since my embedded software class, so this was fun to get back into. All the buttons were just key switches I soldered to the usb encoder connector wires. I used both brown and white Kailh switches for every button on the controller. It took a bit to get any solid soldering done because I had to basically relearn it. Not every joint is perfect, but at least they all work properly. 
<img src="/pictures/keys.jpg">
<img src="/pictures/guitar3.jpg">


Next up was the neck. This was trickier because the wires that connect to the usb encoder aren't very long on their own, so I needed some wires that would reach and use them to bridge the gap between the usb encoder and the key switches. I cut up an old ethernet cable and used the wires inside there. It netted me 4 pairs of wires that I could use, and I had more than enough to cut one down to get the 5th button wired properly. 

<img src="/pictures/guitar5.jpg">

I fed the cables through the neck and bolted everything together. The fret buttons sit nicely above the switches in the neck, and the strum bar sits between two white switches. It ended up looking pretty sleek in black.

<img src="/pictures/guitar6.jpg">

It's not a perfect project though. The fret buttons stick sometimes. I fixed the majority of the issue by sanding down the edges, but there are times where the blue or the orange will get stuck and I have to press them again to unstick them; it's annoying, but it's playable. I can continue to sand them down if I wanted to, so it's not a big deal. The real issue is the strum bar. Once it is played, it will immediatly stay in that position. If I strum down, it stays down. If I strum up, it stays up. I have to manually reset it to the middle if I want it to go back to neutral. This is a bigger problem because it throws me off when I'm playing, and I'm not certain how to fix it. I feel like it could be a friction problem keeping it from returning because it's just plastic pressing against plastic to keep the strum bar in place. I want to find a solution, but for now it's a playable guitar controller whenever I get the feeling I wanna play some Clone Hero on my computer.
